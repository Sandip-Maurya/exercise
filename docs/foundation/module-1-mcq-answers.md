# Foundations Module 1 MCQ Test — Answer Key

Companion to [`module-1-mcq.md`](module-1-mcq.md).

**Duration:** 1 hour · **Questions:** 25 · **Level:** Difficult

### Q1 — **A**

$\alpha+\beta=3$, $\alpha\beta=1$. Then $\alpha^2+\beta^2=(\alpha+\beta)^2-2\alpha\beta=7$, and
$$
\alpha^4+\beta^4=(\alpha^2+\beta^2)^2-2\alpha^2\beta^2=49-2=47.
$$

### Q2 — **B**

Split at $x=0,1,3$. Solving on each piece yields the three roots
$$
x=2-\sqrt{6},\qquad x=2,\qquad x=1+\sqrt{5}.
$$

### Q3 — **A**

For $x\neq\pm 2$,
$$
\dfrac{(x-2)(x-3)}{(x-2)(x+2)}=\dfrac{x-3}{x+2}\leq 0 \quad (x\neq 2),
$$
so $x\in(-2,3]\setminus\{2\}=(-2,2)\cup(2,3]$. (At $x=2$ the original expression is undefined.)

### Q4 — **A**

Domain $x>1$. Then $(x-1)(x+3)=8\Rightarrow x^2+2x-11=0\Rightarrow x=-1\pm 2\sqrt{3}$. Only $-1+2\sqrt{3}>1$.

### Q5 — **A**

$a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}=6n+2$ for $n\geq 2$, and $a_1=8$. Thus $a_{10}=62$.  
(Equivalently $a_n=6n+2$ for all $n$ after checking $a_1$.)

### Q6 — **B**

Put $t=3^x>0$: $t^2-3t-54=0\Rightarrow(t-9)(t+6)=0\Rightarrow t=9\Rightarrow x=2$.

### Q7 — **A**

Roots are $a\pm 1$. Need $a-1>0$ and $a+1>0$, i.e. $a>1$. (At $a=1$ one root is $0$, not positive.)

### Q8 — **C**

$$
S=\sum_{k=2}^{9}\log_\pi k=\log_\pi(2\cdot 3\cdots 9)=\log_\pi(9!)=\dfrac{\ln 9!}{\ln\pi}\approx 11.18\in(11,12).
$$

### Q9 — **B**

$\sin^2 x=1-\sin x\Rightarrow\cos^2 x=\sin x$. Then $\cos^2 x\in[0,1]$ and $\sin x\geq 0$. The expression is $(\cos^2 x+1)^3\cdot\cos^{0}$ wait:
$$
\cos^{12}x+3\cos^{10}x+3\cos^8x+\cos^6x=(\cos^2 x+1)^3\cdot ? 
$$
Actually $=\cos^6 x(\cos^2 x+1)^3$? Check: $\cos^6(\cos^6+3\cos^4+3\cos^2+1)=(\cos^2+1)^3\cos^6$. Yes.
Since $\cos^2 x=\sin x$ and $\sin x+\sin^2 x=1\Rightarrow\sin x(1+\sin x)=1$. Also $\cos^2 x=\sin x$, so $\sin x=\cos^2 x$. From $\sin x=1-\sin^2 x=(1-\sin x)(1+\sin x)$.  
Easier: $\sin x=1-\sin^2 x\Rightarrow\sin x=\cos^2 x$. Then $\cos^2 x+\cos^4 x=\cos^2 x(1+\cos^2 x)=1$? From original $\sin+\sin^2=1$ and $\sin=\cos^2$: $\cos^2+\cos^4=1$.  
Then expression $=(\cos^2 x)^6+3(\cos^2)^5+3(\cos^2)^4+(\cos^2)^3=(\cos^2)^3(\cos^2+1)^3=(\cos^2(\cos^2+1))^3=1^3=1$.

### Q10 — **A**

$\sin 5x=\sin 3x$ gives $x=n\pi$ or $x=\dfrac{(2n+1)\pi}{8}$. In $(0,\pi)$ only the four values $\dfrac{\pi}{8},\dfrac{3\pi}{8},\dfrac{5\pi}{8},\dfrac{7\pi}{8}$.

### Q11 — **A**

Put $u=\sin x\in[-1,1]$. Then $g(u)=u^2+u+1$ has a minimum at $u=-\tfrac12$ with $g=\tfrac34$, and $g(1)=3$. Continuous image: $\bigl[\tfrac34,\,3\bigr]$.

### Q12 — **C**

$\sqrt{3}\sin x-\cos x=2\sin\!\bigl(x-\tfrac{\pi}{6}\bigr)=\sqrt{2}$, so $\sin\!\bigl(x-\tfrac{\pi}{6}\bigr)=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.  
Hence $x-\tfrac{\pi}{6}=2n\pi+\tfrac{\pi}{4}$ or $2n\pi+\tfrac{3\pi}{4}$, giving $x=2n\pi+\tfrac{5\pi}{12}$ or $2n\pi+\tfrac{11\pi}{12}$.

### Q13 — **D**

$\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\dfrac{\tfrac12+\tfrac13}{1-\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac13}=1$ with $\alpha+\beta\in\bigl(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\bigr)$, so $\alpha+\beta=\tfrac{\pi}{4}$. Then $\sin\!\bigl(2(\alpha+\beta)\bigr)=\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2}=1$.

### Q14 — **B**

$\dfrac{8\pi}{5}=2\pi-\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$, and $\sin\!\bigl(2\pi-\tfrac{2\pi}{5}\bigr)=-\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$. Principal value: $-\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$.

### Q15 — **B**

$\cot^{-1}1=\tan^{-1}1=\tfrac{\pi}{4}$. Also $\tan^{-1}\tfrac12+\tan^{-1}\tfrac13=\tfrac{\pi}{4}$. Total $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$.

### Q16 — **B**

$\tan\!\bigl(\tan^{-1}x+\tan^{-1}y\bigr)=1=\dfrac{x+y}{1-xy}$ (valid since $xy<1$), so $x+y-xy=1$.

### Q17 — **A**

$\sin\alpha=\tfrac35$, $\cos\alpha=\tfrac45$. Then
$$
\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2}=\dfrac{\sin\alpha}{1+\cos\alpha}=\dfrac{3/5}{1+4/5}=\dfrac{1}{3}.
$$

### Q18 — **A**

$\dfrac{x-1}{2-x}\geq 0\Rightarrow 1\leq x<2$; arcsin argument always in $[-1,1]$; $\log(4-x)$ needs $x<4$. Intersection: $[1,2)$.

### Q19 — **A**

Set $y=f(x)$ and require a real $x$: discriminant condition gives $\dfrac13\leq y\leq 3$.

### Q20 — **C**

$[\,x\,]=4$ or $[\,x\,]=-1$, so $x\in[4,5)\cup[-1,0)$ — infinitely many solutions.

### Q21 — **B**

Need $4-g(x)\geq 0\Rightarrow x^2-5x+2\leq 0\Rightarrow x\in\bigl[\tfrac{5-\sqrt{17}}{2},\tfrac{5+\sqrt{17}}{2}\bigr]$.

### Q22 — **C**

Not one-one (e.g. $f(-1)=0=f(0)$). Range: $(-\infty,1)\cup[0,4]\cup(-\infty,3)=(-\infty,4]$.

### Q23 — **A**

By inclusion–exclusion: $3^4-\binom{3}{1}2^4+\binom{3}{2}1^4=81-48+3=36$.

### Q24 — **B**

$\{x\}$ has period $1$ and $\{x/2\}$ has period $2$, so the sum has period $2$. The others are not periodic ($\sqrt{2}$ incommensurate with $1$; $x^2$ arguments destroy periodicity).

### Q25 — **A**

Solve $y=\dfrac{e^x-1}{e^x+1}$ for $x$: $e^x=\dfrac{1+y}{1-y}$ ($|y|<1$), so $x=\dfrac12\ln\dfrac{1+y}{1-y}$.
